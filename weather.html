Long Bay

The wind.

Kiteable from early November until early September. The stronger, more consistent wind is June through mid-August. Long Bay faces the east-southeast trades, so the wind comes from the side and slightly onshore. You stay in the bay. On a normal tide you can stand.

Early November – May

Kiteable.

The season is open from early November. A winter month averages about 21 good kite days. These are not the strongest months.

June – mid-August

The best wind.

June through mid-August is the strongest and the most consistent. Rain comes in short bursts. It is hot, and the breeze is why you come.

Early September

The season closes.

Kiteable days run through early September. After that the trades drop until they return in early November. Ask before you plan a trip around a lesson.

How much wind

About 12 knots to ride.

Under 12 knots

We do not run a lesson under 12 knots. No beach theory, and no hour on the sand with nothing in the air. If you are not going to fly a full-size kite, we will not take the time or the money. A ground lesson on a no-wind day is a way to charge someone who does not know better. We will not do that. Hunter calls about an hour before. Full refund if the lesson is cancelled for weather.

12 to 18 knots

The lesson window. Enough power to ride, not so much that a first water start is a fight. Most teaching days sit here.

18 to 25 knots

A smaller kite. Still a good coached lesson if the wind is steady. You spend more of the hour on control.

Above 25 knots

We discourage lessons above 25 knots. It is intimidating, and a first water start in that much wind is a bad idea. Advanced lessons can still go out when the rider already handles a small kite and the wind is steady.

Water stays warm enough for board shorts all year. Winter is about the high 70s °F. Summer is the low to mid 80s. A rash guard is the useful extra. Most riders here are on a 10 to 14 metre kite. The school brings the size for the wind that morning.
