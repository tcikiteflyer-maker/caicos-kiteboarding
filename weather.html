Long Bay

The wind.

Long Bay faces the east-southeast trades, so the wind comes from the side and slightly onshore. You stay in the bay. The water is warm, and on a normal tide you can stand.

December – May

Book this.

The steadiest months. East to southeast, often 15 to 20 knots. This is when a first lesson is easiest to plan.

June – August

Still riding.

The trades often hold, and some of the stronger days are in summer. Rain comes in short bursts. It is hot, and the breeze makes it workable.

September – October

The quiet months.

The trades ease off, and this is hurricane season. There are still kite days. There are also weeks that are not worth flying. Ask before you book a trip around a lesson.

How much wind

About 12 knots to ride.

Under 12 knots

The beach part of a first lesson can still happen. Riding is unlikely unless you are light and on a big kite. If the day will not work, Hunter calls about an hour before. Full refund if the lesson is cancelled for weather.

12 to 18 knots

The lesson window. Enough power to ride, not so much that a first water start is a fight. Most teaching days sit here.

18 to 25 knots

A smaller kite. Still a good coached lesson if the wind is steady. You spend more of the hour on control.

Above 25 knots

For riders who already handle a small kite. Beginners wait for a kinder day.

Water stays warm enough for board shorts all year. Winter is about the high 70s °F. Summer is the low to mid 80s. A rash guard is the useful extra. Most days on this beach are a 9 to 12 metre kite. The school brings the size for the wind that morning.
